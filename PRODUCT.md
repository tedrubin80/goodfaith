# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Indie record label owners and operators running 5-50 signed artists, currently managing royalties across 2+ distributors on Google Sheets. They're evaluating this page pre-launch to decide whether the product is trustworthy enough to hand their financial and catalog data to. They are skeptical by default — burned before by platforms that lock in distribution, take a cut of earnings, or could vanish and strand their catalog. The job to be done on this page: decide "can I trust this enough to join the waitlist."

## Product Purpose

Good Faith Record Management is a flat-fee, distributor-agnostic record label management platform. This marketing page exists to convert pre-launch visitors (from Reddit, word of mouth, direct link) into waitlist signups by establishing credibility before the product itself is ready to demo. Success = a visitor who arrived skeptical of "another SaaS tool" leaves believing this one is different, and gives their email.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, transparent, expert-calm. The overriding value is trust — this needs to read like a serious financial/ops tool a label's finance person would hand their royalty statements to, not a flashy consumer music app. Comfortable rather than hyped: confident and settled, not urgent or salesy. Closer to Mercury/Ramp-style banking trust than Spotify-for-Artists gloss or DistroKid/TuneCore DIY energy.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS cream/gradient template: cream or off-white body background, gradient text, tiny uppercase eyebrow labels above every section, hero-metric-with-gradient-accent pattern, identical icon+heading+text card grids.
- Flashy DIY consumer music app energy (DistroKid/TuneCore-style bright, youthful, gamified) — this audience has been burned by exactly that tone and it undercuts trust.
- Cold enterprise-SaaS-for-enterprises look (Synchtank/Labelcamp-style inaccessible, sales-gated, no visible pricing) — this brand is self-serve and transparent, not gatekept.

## Design Principles

1. **Trust is shown, not claimed.** Every section should give evidence (verbatim community quotes, explicit pricing, explicit data-export guarantees) rather than asserting "we're trustworthy" in copy.
2. **Comfortable confidence over hype.** No urgency tactics, no countdown timers, no "act now" pressure. The tone is a calm expert who has already solved your problem.
3. **Transparency as a visual habit, not just a value.** Pricing, data portability, and the mechanics of the royalty hub should be shown plainly (real numbers, real steps) rather than abstracted behind marketing language.
4. **Speak the customer's own words back to them.** The verbatim Reddit quotes are the most credible copy available — use them as structural social proof, not decoration.
5. **Professional, not sterile.** Expert-calm should not collapse into generic-corporate; the page should feel specific to the music-label domain (ISRC/UPC/royalty vocabulary used correctly and confidently).

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA: ≥4.5:1 contrast for body text, ≥3:1 for large text, full keyboard navigation, `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives for all motion, no color-only signaling.
